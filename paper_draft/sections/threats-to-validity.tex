\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\noindent\textbf{External Validity.} We mine only publicly visible artifacts. Security issues are often disclosed privately and resolved under embargo, leaving just a
released advisory or changelog entry in public; SecDevQA captures only questions negotiated in the open and may under-represent privately-routed ones. We scope
our claims to publicly resolved security Q\&A. Repositories are chosen for security activity and popularity (\dnum{three} languages so far), and the hard-verifiable filter
may exclude question types lacking checkable facts. We report per-repository results and derive the taxonomy from the full corpus to keep both gaps visible.

\noindent\textbf{Construct Validity.} Our evaluation questions are normalized from raw threads to stand alone and to withhold the resolution, which could weaken the authenticity claim or leak the answer. We address both by construction (\S\ref{sec:normalization}): the verbatim original reporter comment is retained and released with every normalized question; a deterministic check flags any fix-type identifier that appears in a question without the reporter having stated it before the answer; and every normalized pair is independently reviewed by two researchers, so inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's $\kappa$) on the knowledge-type and leak judgments is reported over the full set and disagreements are reconciled before evaluation. Because the gold answer is distilled strictly from the thread, a terse maintainer pointer yields a terse gold answer; we label these honestly and, for grounded pairs that cite a fix pull request or commit, supply the resolved diff to the judge under the with-context conditions rather than inflating the ground truth. The parametric/grounded label further guards the no-context condition: scoring a model on a repository-internal fact it cannot know unaided would measure memorization, so only parametric pairs anchor no-context capability claims.

\noindent\textbf{Internal Validity.} Free-text grading uses an LLM judge. Hard facts ground but do not eliminate judge error; we verify named claims, cross-check identifiers, and re-evaluate a \dnum{15\%} sample by hand. Public threads may be memorized~\cite{stackrepoqa2025}; the no-context condition with training-cutoff stratification flags this.
